# Where the new content comes from

**How it was made.** Everything was researched from public sources, then written in our own words. Facts (dates, places, events) are free to use; other people's wording and photographs are not, so none is copied. Every story ends with a "Sources and credits" list, and each source is linked there.

**One exception, and why it is allowed.** John Henry Patterson's *The Man-Eaters of Tsavo* (1907) is in the public domain (Patterson died in 1947; the book is on Project Gutenberg). One 17-word sentence from it is quoted with credit.

**What it is not.** It is not a substitute for local review. Where sources disagree (the victims of the Tsavo lions, who named tsavorite, the details of Mwangeka's last battle) the story says so. Please have a local editor read each story before launch, and add or correct anything the Taita community knows better.

## Places

Positions are given **only where a source gives one**: Mzima Springs (NGA GeoNames) and Tsavo East National Park (its centre, from the park's reference entry). Voi War Cemetery uses Voi town's centre and is marked approximate. Every other place has no position yet, so it is listed but not on the map: add one in the admin area once you have it checked on the ground.

## What was left out on purpose

- **Sponsors and partners.** Showing a real company as your sponsor or partner without their agreement would be false. `docs/sponsor-prospects.md` lists real organisations active in Taita Taveta that you could approach.
- **Living people.** No living person is profiled or named in a story. The people in the stories are historical figures. Living people appear only as credited sources, for something they have published or said publicly (for example, a chief's written records of Mwangeka, or a researcher's book on the First World War in Tsavo).
- **Photographs.** None were copied. The stories use the site's existing stand-in pictures: replace them with photographs you own or have permission for.
- **Sacred sites.** The Taita skull caves and shrines are sacred. The stories mention them respectfully and give no directions.
